MotionSense-IMU

My first custom PCB: a 6-axis IMU board designed from a blank schematic, routed in KiCad, sent through fabrication and assembly, and now in hardware bring-up.

01 /

starting from a blank schematic

I wanted a hardware project where I owned the whole path, not just the firmware after someone else had already designed the board. MotionSense started as a blank KiCad project and a simple goal: power an IMU from USB-C and expose everything a microcontroller needs to talk to it.

The result is a small breakout board around the ST LSM6DSM, a 6-axis accelerometer and gyroscope. It takes 5 V from USB-C, regulates it to 3.3 V, and breaks out power, I²C, and the sensor's INT1 signal through a 5-pin header.

02 /

the architecture

I kept Rev A intentionally small: one power path, one sensor, and one clean interface to an external MCU. That made the board manageable enough to design from scratch while still forcing me to think through power, pull-ups, decoupling, routing, fabrication, and bring-up.

inputUSB-C5 V power input
regulationAP2112K3.3 V LDO
sensingLSM6DSMaccelerometer + gyro
interface5-pin header3V3 · GND · SDA · SCL · INT1
03 /

schematic decisions

The schematic is small, but every part has a job. I used an AP2112K-3.3 regulator for the 3.3 V rail, 4.7 kΩ pull-ups on SDA and SCL, and local decoupling around both the regulator and IMU.

LSM6DSM6-axis accelerometer + gyroscope
AP2112K-3.35 V to 3.3 V regulation
4.7 kΩ × 2I²C pull-ups for SDA + SCL
5-pin header3V3 · GND · SDA · SCL · INT1

The external-microcontroller approach was deliberate. Rev A is a sensor board first, so I can bring up the IMU independently and connect it to whatever controller makes sense for the next version.

04 /

layout, routing, and checks

I laid out the board as a two-layer design in KiCad. I experimented with Quilter and Freerouting during routing, then brought the result back into KiCad for cleanup and final checks.

The finished design passes KiCad DRC with 0 violations and 0 unconnected items. I also generated the Gerber and drill files and checked them in GerbView before sending the board out.

05 /

from KiCad to a real board

I generated the Gerbers, drill files, BOM, and component-position data for manufacturing, then sent the design to JLCPCB. The order gave me five boards total, with two assembled.

This was the part where the project stopped being a clean CAD file and became actual hardware I could probe, debug, and potentially revise.

06 /

bring-up

The boards are back from fabrication, so Rev A is now in bring-up. I’m checking the power path first, then I²C communication, then the sensor itself before moving on to a driver.

  • donefabrication + assembly — physical Rev A boards are in hand
  • checkingpower rails — verify USB 5 V input and regulated 3.3 V
  • nextI²C detection — confirm the LSM6DSM responds at its configured address
  • nextsensor identity — read WHO_AM_I and verify communication
  • nextlive data — stream accelerometer and gyroscope readings
  • nextdriver + INT1 — write the interface and test the interrupt pin
